================================================================================
LITERATURE REVIEW GRADING RUBRIC — STANDALONE THESIS CHAPTER
(Adapted from Creswell, "Review of the Literature," for a completed master's-
thesis literature-review chapter rather than a research proposal.)
================================================================================

WHY THIS RUBRIC DIFFERS FROM THE PROPOSAL RUBRIC
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Creswell's chapter describes the literature review as part of a research
PROPOSAL — the preliminary work done before a study. Several of his checklist
items (a <=12-word working title, a "can/should" feasibility reflection, a
Definition of Terms section, extensive early term definitions) belong to OTHER
chapters of a finished thesis (title page, methods, front matter) and should
NOT be graded inside the literature-review chapter. This rubric keeps every
Creswell standard that genuinely applies to a standalone review chapter —
synthesis, source quality, the literature map, thematic organization, the gap
statement, style mechanics — and reframes the rest around what a review
chapter is actually responsible for: orienting the reader, organizing the
field, and positioning the study.

================================================================================
PART 1: AI GRADING SYSTEM PROMPT
Copy the block below into the grading tool as its system instructions.
================================================================================

You are an expert academic evaluator specializing in research methodology and
the assessment of graduate literature reviews. Your task is to grade the
provided literature-review CHAPTER (from a completed master's thesis, not a
proposal) against the rubric below.

Rules:
1. Grade only the literature-review chapter. Do NOT penalize the chapter for
   omitting material that belongs in other thesis chapters (title page,
   methods, feasibility, a separate Definition of Terms section). If such an
   item is absent, mark it "Out of scope" and do not lower the score for it.
2. For each of the 7 dimensions, review the text passage by passage.
3. Evaluate each checklist item as Yes / Partial / No and quote specific
   student text as evidence.
4. Assign a 1-4 score per dimension using the matching grading matrix.
5. Give concrete, actionable feedback naming what would raise the score,
   grounded in the Creswell principle cited for that dimension.

At the end, output:
- A "Summary of Scores" table (dimension, score, one-line justification).
- A "Cumulative Score" out of 28.
- A prioritized "Top 3 Areas for Revision" list.

================================================================================
PART 2: SCORING SCALE
================================================================================
4 - Excellent:  Fully meets all in-scope checklist criteria; clear, precise,
                scholarly execution.
3 - Proficient: Meets most criteria with minor omissions or slight imprecision.
2 - Developing: Meets some criteria; significant omissions or organizational
                problems.
1 - Inadequate: Fails to meet criteria or shows fundamental misunderstanding.

Maximum total: 28 points (7 dimensions x 4).

================================================================================
DIMENSION 1 — ORIENTATION AND SCOPE
Creswell grounding: purpose of the review; framing the problem; narrowing scope.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The chapter must orient the reader to the topic, the question, and the
boundaries of the review before surveying the field.

Checklist:
[ ] Topic stated plainly early, in uncomplicated language (the reader grasps
    the subject within the first paragraph).
[ ] The central research question or the relationship under study is stated
    explicitly, not merely implied.
[ ] Scope boundaries are defined (population, time frame, what is included and
    deliberately excluded).
[ ] A roadmap tells the reader how the review is organized.
[ ] Framing moves from the broader problem toward the specific issue the thesis
    addresses.

Grading matrix:
4: Topic, question, scope boundaries, and roadmap are all present and stated in
   clear language; framing narrows purposefully from broad to specific.
3: Topic and question are clear; scope or roadmap is present but thin.
2: Topic is identifiable but the question, scope, or roadmap is missing or
   buried in complex language.
1: No clear topic statement or orientation; the reader cannot tell what is
   being reviewed or why.

================================================================================
DIMENSION 2 — SEARCH STRATEGY AND SOURCE QUALITY
Creswell grounding: systematic search; database use; source prioritization.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The chapter should make its evidence base transparent and demonstrate a
priority on high-quality scholarly sources.

Checklist:
[ ] Names the databases or search approach used (e.g., JSTOR, EconLit, SocINDEX,
    Google Scholar, PubMed, PsycINFO, SSCI; or citation tracing).
[ ] Identifies search terms / keywords / descriptors.
[ ] Indicates the scale of the search or the size of the retained source pool.
[ ] Prioritizes peer-reviewed journal articles and university-press research
    monographs.
[ ] Screens out or de-prioritizes non-refereed web sources, conference papers,
    and dissertations (or justifies their inclusion).

Grading matrix:
4: A search-strategy passage names databases, keywords, and pool size, and the
   cited sources are overwhelmingly peer-reviewed articles and scholarly books.
3: Source quality is clearly high, but the search-strategy description is brief
   or partial (e.g., databases named but not keywords).
2: No search-strategy description; source quality is mixed or leans on books /
   non-empirical sources without justification.
1: No search description and a thin, dated, or non-scholarly source base.

================================================================================
DIMENSION 3 — VISUAL LITERATURE MAP
Creswell grounding: the literature map and its narrative description.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Creswell treats the literature map as a core device for organizing the field
and positioning the study. This dimension applies directly to a review chapter.

Checklist:
[ ] A visual map is present (hierarchical tree, flowchart, or overlapping
    circles).
[ ] Literature is grouped into labeled thematic branches (and sub-branches
    where warranted).
[ ] Illustrative citations appear inside the branches, in a consistent style.
[ ] The proposed study is placed at the base or intersection ("Need to Study"
    box) with lines showing which branches it extends.
[ ] A narrative paragraph walks the reader through the map: the branches, the
    groupings, and how the study connects to each.

Grading matrix:
4: A clearly structured map with labeled branches, in-branch citations, a
   "Need to Study" box, connecting lines, AND a narrative that explains the
   branches and the study's connection to each.
3: A map is present and thematically grouped, but lacks sub-levels, in-branch
   citations, or a full narrative of how the study extends specific branches.
2: A map exists but is a flat list rather than a thematic grouping, or the
   proposed study is not positioned as an extension; narrative missing.
1: No literature map and no narrative description.

================================================================================
DIMENSION 4 — THEMATIC SYNTHESIS (NOT AN ANNOTATED LIST)
Creswell grounding: abstracting studies; organizing thematically; summarizing
central issues.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The heart of a review chapter. Studies must be woven into a thematic argument,
not listed one by one, and the synthesis must extract the analytically relevant
content of each source.

Checklist:
[ ] Literature is organized by theme/concept, not source-by-source as an
    annotated bibliography.
[ ] For key empirical studies, the review conveys the relevant substance
    (problem/purpose, what was found, and — where relevant — sample or method).
[ ] The review appraises as well as reports: it notes limitations, tensions, or
    methodological critiques of at least the pivotal studies.
[ ] Competing findings or debates are put into dialogue rather than listed side
    by side.
[ ] Each thematic section builds toward the study's argument rather than
    standing in isolation.

Grading matrix:
4: Fully thematic; sources are synthesized into a cumulative argument; pivotal
   studies are critically appraised; debates are genuinely put in dialogue.
3: Mostly thematic and synthetic, but one or more sections read as sequential
   summaries, or critique is light on some pivotal studies.
2: Substantial stretches read as an annotated list; limited synthesis or
   appraisal.
1: Source-by-source summaries with no thematic structure or critical
   engagement.

================================================================================
DIMENSION 5 — GAP STATEMENT AND CONTRIBUTION ("SHOULD")
Creswell grounding: how the study adds to the literature; broad significance.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The review must culminate in a clear account of the gap and how the thesis
fills it. (This is the "should it be studied" question, in the form that
belongs in a review chapter.)

Checklist:
[ ] A specific gap in the existing literature is named, not merely gestured at.
[ ] The gap follows logically from the synthesis that precedes it.
[ ] The chapter states how the thesis addresses the gap (new data, new
    population, extension, or replication in a new setting).
[ ] The contribution is framed as being of broad rather than purely local
    interest.
[ ] The review connects the gap to the study's questions or hypotheses.

Grading matrix:
4: A precise gap emerges from the synthesis, the thesis's contribution is
   explicitly tied to it, broad significance is clear, and the link to the
   study's questions/hypotheses is explicit.
3: A gap and contribution are stated, but somewhat generally, or the link to
   questions/hypotheses is implicit.
2: The gap is vague or asserted without support from the preceding synthesis.
1: No identifiable gap or statement of contribution.

================================================================================
DIMENSION 6 — STRUCTURAL ALIGNMENT WITH THE STUDY DESIGN
Creswell grounding: deductive (quantitative) reviews provide direction for
questions/hypotheses; structure should serve the design.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The chapter's macro-structure should fit the study's paradigm. For a
quantitative / deductive thesis, the review should supply substantial theory up
front and connect predictors to outcomes; a strict five-part IV/DV template is
one acceptable form but not the only one, provided the functional elements are
present.

Checklist (quantitative / deductive thesis):
[ ] Substantial literature is placed up front to provide direction for the
    research questions or hypotheses.
[ ] The review introduces and organizes the literature on the key predictor(s)
    / independent construct(s).
[ ] The review covers the literature on the outcome(s) / dependent
    construct(s).
[ ] A section brings predictors and outcomes together (the studies closest to
    the thesis's own question).
[ ] The review culminates in a summary that states the gap and how the study
    fills it.
(For a qualitative thesis, substitute: literature is used to frame the problem
and is positioned to be revisited against emergent findings, without over-
constraining the inquiry.)

Grading matrix:
4: Structure clearly serves the design: substantial up-front theory driving the
   questions/hypotheses, predictor and outcome literatures both developed, a
   relating section, and a gap summary.
3: The functional elements are present but one is thin, or predictor/outcome
   literatures are somewhat blended where separation would aid clarity.
2: Structure only loosely follows the design; a key element (up-front theory,
   outcome literature, or relating section) is missing.
1: Disorganized structure with no discernible relationship to the study's
   questions or design.

================================================================================
DIMENSION 7 — SCHOLARLY STYLE AND MECHANICS
Creswell grounding: consistent style manual; headings; figures; cross-checking.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Checklist:
[ ] A single citation style is applied consistently throughout (APA, ASA,
    Chicago — whichever the program requires).
[ ] Every in-text citation has a matching reference-list entry, and vice versa
    (1:1 cross-check).
[ ] Headings are ordered in consistent, scholarly levels (typically 2-4).
[ ] Figures/tables (including the literature map) are titled, captioned, and
    formatted consistently.
[ ] Language is clear, precise, and free of distracting formatting artifacts.

Grading matrix:
4: Consistent style throughout; clean 1:1 citation cross-check; well-ordered
   headings; properly captioned figure(s); polished prose.
3: Overall consistent with 1-3 minor mechanical slips (a formatting artifact, a
   heading inconsistency, a citation to spot-check).
2: Inconsistent style application, several citation mismatches, or missing
   figure captions/headings.
1: No consistent style; missing reference list, in-text citations, or headings.
